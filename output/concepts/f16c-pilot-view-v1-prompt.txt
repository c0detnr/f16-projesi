Use case: stylized-concept.
Create one high-quality wide landscape 16:9 concept image depicting the intended completed desktop flight simulator from a Turkish user's F-16C Block 52 Blender project.
Strict first-person pilot-eye view from the single seat, looking forward and slightly down, comfortably wide perspective without fisheye. The upper 45 percent shows clear bubble canopy and a sunlit runway ahead with low dry hills in distance. The lower 55 percent shows the front instrument panel and visible portions of both side consoles.
Art direction: VTOL VR-inspired economical low-poly game geometry, but more realistic proportions and softly lit matte materials, clean readable shapes, modest bevels, subdued texture wear. Clearly a polished achievable real-time game render, not a photograph or ultra-detailed cinematic DCS render.
Cockpit design target: recognizable traditional F-16C layout, compact green transparent HUD above central ICP keypad; exactly two square MFDs left and right with physical bezel buttons, small green DED display to upper right of ICP, supporting round analog flight instruments and engine indicators; narrow single-seat cockpit; right side-stick at right console and throttle at left console, NO center stick, NO yoke. Dark charcoal panels, medium-gray sidewalls, small English panel labels, distinct toggle switches and rotary knobs for future clickable controls. Powered-on green avionics with simple navigation/flight indications, no combat imagery. No giant modern panoramic display. Bubble canopy has subtle reflections and unobstructed forward visibility.
Camera at seated pilot eyes, not outside the aircraft and not looking at the pilot. No pilot body or hands necessary. Single uninterrupted scene, no collage, no captions, no logos, no watermark, no UI overlays. Balanced daylight exposure keeps all cockpit panels readable. This is visual exploration based on a plan, not an engineering diagram. Do not invent Turkish text inside the cockpit; the project's Turkish exterior livery is outside this view.
